Strong online interest that is not turning into showings

The listing preparation, photography, 3D tour, and floor plan have done exactly what they were designed to do. The home has drawn 881 views on Homes.com and 392 on Zillow, and buyers are saving it at a very high rate. We also had a good amount of showing activity in the first week. That leaves us in a tough spot. Buyers clearly like what they see online, but at the current price they are not taking the next step.

How Zillow buyers are responding

The listing has generated 392 Zillow views and 57 saves in 14 days. About 14.5% of viewers have saved the property, roughly one out of every seven visitors. That is the strongest conversion among the nearby listings we track, and well above the 10% level I look for in a listing that is building real momentum.

The home is averaging 28 Zillow views per day, well ahead of the listings that have been on the market longer. Online, the home is doing its job. The gap is between saving the home and scheduling a showing, and that is where price comes in.

Why the rate environment matters

The recent rise in mortgage rates has been meaningful. Freddie Mac reported that the national average 30-year fixed mortgage rate reached 7.03% on September 24, up from 6.95% one week earlier and 6.66% on August 27. That is the first reading above 7% since January 2025. Individual loan quotes will vary, but buyers in this price range are especially payment-sensitive. When rates move higher quickly, buyers reduce their price ceiling, delay showings, or become more selective about which homes they tour.

That is what I am seeing right now. Buyers at this price point are pulling back and dropping the price range they search in. As a rough illustration, the monthly principal and interest payment that covered a $420,000 purchase at late-August rates now covers a purchase closer to $404,000.

You can see it in the market data too. In our search area, no home has gone under contract since September 15, the same week rates started climbing toward 7%.

My honest opinion is that a realistic sale price right now may be around $400,000. That is based on current buyer activity, how the market has moved this fall, and the number of comparable homes sitting on the market without going under contract. 9396 Rose Court, the same size as your home and in the same Park Vista neighborhood, closed on September 23 at $410,000, but with $11,000 in seller concessions. That left a net price of $399,000. Two more same-size homes nearby are now listed at $400,000.

With the online activity not converting into showings, I recommend adjusting the price to $410,000 and giving it a couple of weeks at that price. A price change sends a fresh alert to the 57 buyers who saved the home on Zillow and gives them a reason to take another look.

We may be heading toward a $400,000 list price. I want to be upfront about that now so it is not a surprise later. If $410,000 does not bring showings back within a couple of weeks, the market is likely telling us that $400,000 is where buyers will act, and I would recommend we be prepared to make that move.

Getting ahead of the market now matters. There is still buyer activity today. Once the holidays arrive, buyer activity typically slows considerably. Adjusting now gives us the best chance to reach the buyers who are still actively looking before that happens.

I know this is not the number any of us hoped for when we listed, and the decision is yours. I would rather recommend the move I believe will get your home sold than wait and find ourselves in the same position as the holidays approach.

Next week we will be watching whether showing requests return after the adjustment, along with Zillow views per day and save rate against the same competitors.

What the comparison tells us

The most useful comparison is 9396 Rose Court: same size, same neighborhood, and a net price of $399,000 after concessions. Two newer same-size listings, 9390 Nagel Drive and 9051 Lilly Court, are asking $400,000, and 1070 Elm Place went under contract at $389,000. The one home at $425,000 that went pending, 1241 E 90th Avenue, is fully renovated with a new roof, HVAC, and sewer line, and it went under contract on September 15 before rates moved above 7%.

Meanwhile, homes priced near us are sitting. 1730 Hoffman Way has been on the market for 67 days at $418,500, and 1050 Russell Boulevard was withdrawn after 194 days, last listed at $415,000. The pattern suggests buyers are acting around $400,000 and passing on homes priced above it.

Why waiting for spring is not guaranteed to help

Spring often brings more buyers, but it also usually brings more competing listings. If mortgage rates remain elevated or buyer confidence softens further, the seasonal increase in activity may not produce a better result. I do not want to create unnecessary urgency, but I also do not think we should assume that waiting will automatically improve our position. Acting now, while buyers are still active and before the holiday slowdown, gives us the best chance to turn this online interest into showings.
